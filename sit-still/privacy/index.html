Privacy Policy

Sit Still for iPhone and Android. Last updated 17 September 2026.

Sit Still collects nothing. It has no account, no sign-in and no server of ours. Nothing you do in the app is transmitted anywhere, because there is nowhere for it to go.

This policy covers both versions of Sit Still — the iPhone app and the Android app. They are built separately and each uses its own platform’s frameworks, so a few sections below apply to one and not the other. Those are labelled.

What stays on your device

  • Your sessions, tasks, notes, whiskers balance, cats and accessories.
  • Your settings, including the focus shield mode and bedtime schedule.
  • The apps you chose to shield.

All of it lives in the app’s own storage on your phone. Deleting the app deletes all of it.

Blocking apps — iPhone

Sit Still uses Apple’s Screen Time frameworks (FamilyControls, ManagedSettings, DeviceActivity) to shield apps during a session and during a bedtime window you set. It reads no Screen Time usage data — not which apps you open, not for how long, not how often. It only raises and lowers shields you asked for, on the device they were asked for. The apps you pick come back from Apple’s own picker as opaque tokens the app cannot read or resolve into names, and are stored in a shared container on the device only.

Blocking apps — Android

Android has no equivalent of Screen Time, so the same feature is built from two permissions you grant yourself in system Settings, and it is worth being precise about what they do.

  • Usage access lets the app ask the system which app came to the front just now. That is the whole of what it reads, it is read only while a shield you switched on is standing, and the answer is compared against your own list and then discarded. Nothing is written down, nothing is added up, and no history of what you use is kept or built.
  • Display over other apps is what lets the app draw its own screen in front of a blocked one.

Both are switches in Android’s own Settings, both can be revoked there at any time, and with either one off the feature simply does not run. The apps you pick are stored on the device only.

Checking the clock

The rooms that appear for one month only are scheduled inside the app, and whether one is open is decided by comparing the date against that schedule. To keep that honest, the app makes one small request over a secure connection and reads the time out of the reply’s standard Date header. On iPhone that request goes to Apple; on Android it goes to the same address Android itself uses to check a connection.

Nothing of yours goes with it. The request has no contents — it asks for headers and no page — carries no identifier, no account and nothing about you or your use of the app, and the reply is thrown away except for a single number: how far your phone’s clock is from the network’s. That number is kept on your phone and nowhere else. As with any request any app or browser makes, the server on the other end sees that a connection happened, under its own operator’s policy.

It happens at most once every fifteen minutes, only while the app is open, and if it fails — you are offline, on a plane, behind a firewall — the app simply uses your phone’s own clock and carries on. Your time zone always comes from your phone.

Reminders — iPhone

With Reminders sync switched on, the app reads and writes its own Reminders list, plus any individual reminder you deliberately linked. That happens through Apple’s EventKit on your device. Nothing is copied off it.

Notifications — Android

The Android app has no Reminders to sync with, so the morning list is a notification the app schedules on your phone instead. It is off unless you turn it on, it is scheduled and delivered entirely on the device, and nothing about it leaves.

Photos

Saving a room as wallpaper, or saving the Look Back picture, writes one image to your photo library when you tap it. On iPhone the permission is add-only: the app cannot read your library. On Android the image is written to your Pictures folder through the system’s own media store, and the app reads nothing back.

Premium

Sit Still Premium is a subscription sold by Apple on the App Store and by Google on Google Play. The purchase happens entirely inside their systems: the app asks the store what is on sale and whether this account already owns it, and it is told yes or no. We never see your name, your email, your payment details or your billing address, and no record of your purchase is kept anywhere but on your device and with the store. Apple and Google handle that transaction under their own privacy policies.

Feedback email

Sending feedback opens your own mail app with a message you can read and edit before sending. It includes a few technical lines — the app version, your phone’s model and Android or iOS version, and a note about anything visibly wrong, such as a permission that was switched on here but not granted by the system. You can see all of it and delete any of it before you send. If you send it, that email reaches sitstill@alteraapps.com and nowhere else. Not sending it means nothing is transmitted at all.

Analytics, ads and third parties

There are none. The app contains no analytics SDK, no advertising SDK, no crash reporter and no third-party code beyond the two store libraries needed to sell and restore the subscription. Nothing is sold, shared or profiled, because nothing is collected.

Apple and Google may collect their own aggregate, anonymised statistics about downloads and crashes under their own privacy policies. Those never reach the developer as anything identifying you.

This website

alteraapps.com is a set of static pages served by GitHub Pages. It sets no cookies and runs no analytics or third-party scripts. GitHub keeps ordinary server logs of requests to it under its own privacy statement.

Children

Sit Still is rated for any age and safe for one, but it is not directed at children and knowingly collects no information from anyone, of any age.

Changes

If a future version ever collects anything, this page will say so before that version ships, and the App Store privacy label and the Google Play Data safety section will be updated to match.
