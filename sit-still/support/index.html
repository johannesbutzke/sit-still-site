Support

Common questions, and how to reach a person.

This page covers both versions of Sit Still — the iPhone app and the Android app. They are built separately, and a few answers below differ between them. Those are labelled.

Something wrong, or an idea?

sitstill@alteraapps.com

From inside the app, Settings → About → Send feedback opens the same address with your version, build, OS version and device model already filled in — which makes anything reproducible much faster to fix. You can read and edit all of it before sending. Expect a reply within a few days.

Common questions

The room stops playing when I lock the screen

It shouldn’t — the room carries on with the screen locked, and the session’s clock carries on whether or not the room is making a sound, so a countdown still finishes and still chimes with the sound switched off. If the room went quiet mid‑session, something interrupted it.

iPhone. A call, an alarm, Siri, or plugging into a car. Opening the app puts it back.

Android. A call or an alarm. Unplugging headphones also stops it, deliberately: the system warns an app when sound is about to switch to the phone’s own speaker, and a room that carried on would be a room playing out loud in a room full of people. Opening the app puts it back.

The room sound stops my music

It shouldn’t. Room sound is deliberately mixed under whatever else is playing rather than interrupting it — on both platforms it never claims the audio for itself. If it is louder than you want under music, Settings → Sounds has a volume slider and a Listen button. On iPhone it ignores the ring/silent switch; on Android it plays on the media volume rather than the ringer.

If the music stops the moment a session starts, look at the focus shield rather than the sound — though the two platforms get there differently.

iPhone. Shielding an app does not just hide it: iOS stops it running, and a music app that has been stopped stops playing.

Android. A covered app keeps running, but the shield takes the phone’s audio outright for as long as the curtain is up, and from Android 12 the system forces whatever held it to fade out and stop. That is on purpose: a video that merely paused would start itself again the moment the block lifted. It also means the music will not resume by itself afterwards.

Either way the fix is the same: Strict shields everything, and Whitelist shields everything you haven’t picked, so your music app needs to be on that list — Settings → Focus shield → Whitelist, then Add apps. Companionship blocks nothing at all. A blocking rule or the bedtime lock that happens to cover it does the same thing a session does.

The quick test: start a session on Companionship with the music playing. If it keeps playing, the shield was the cause rather than the room.

Blocked apps aren’t being blocked

Check Settings → Focus shield first: Companionship is the default and blocks nothing. Whitelist and Strict both need permission the system asks for the first time you pick one, and both say so on their own row when it is missing.

iPhone. The permission is Screen Time access. Screen Time cannot shield some system apps, and a whitelist is capped at ten apps — websites keep Screen Time’s fifty — because past that the system stops honouring the list.

Android. There are two permissions rather than one, both switches in Android’s own Settings: Usage access, which lets the app ask which app just came to the front, and Display over other apps, which lets it draw its own screen in front of one. With either off the shield simply does not run. The whitelist is capped at ten apps here too. Your launcher and system Settings are never covered, on purpose — otherwise the shield would stand in front of the way home and in front of the screen that switches it off.

Apps are still blocked and no session is running

iPhone. Open Sit Still. A shield is a system setting rather than something the app holds open, so it survives a force-quit — and opening the app clears any shield with no session behind it. If you deleted the app while a shield was up, reinstall it, open it once, and the shield comes down.

Android. The curtain is Sit Still’s own screen rather than a system setting, so it cannot outlive the app: force-quitting Sit Still, or uninstalling it, takes the curtain with it. If one keeps coming back, something scheduled is running rather than stuck — a blocking rule or the bedtime lock. Open the app and check Settings → Focus shield.

How do I block apps when I’m not in a session?

Settings → Focus shield → Blocking on a schedule. A rule is one of two things. A schedule puts the apps you choose away between two times, on the days you pick. A time limit gives them so many minutes a day and then puts them away — until tomorrow, or for a stretch you choose. Either way the rule runs whether or not you ever open Sit Still that day.

Eight rules is the most either phone will hold. A scheduled window has to be at least 15 minutes, a daily limit runs from 5 minutes to 6 hours, and a block lasts at least 15 minutes. Rules come with Premium. The bedtime lock is the same machinery pointed at one window a night, and it keeps a screen of its own.

iPhone. A rule covers up to 50 apps, which is Screen Time’s limit rather than the app’s.

A rule or the bedtime lock never fires

On both, a rule with no apps chosen is armed and puts nothing away.

iPhone. iOS refuses to monitor a window shorter than 15 minutes, and the start and end callbacks only arrive while the device is in use — a phone face-down at 22:30 gets its lock when it is next picked up. Both also need Screen Time access.

Android. Rules are alarms, and from Android 13 an exact alarm is a permission of its own: Alarms & reminders, in system Settings. Without it nothing breaks — the app falls back to keeping a quiet standing service instead, which is never less reliable, only more visible in the notification shade. Alarms are also cleared by restarting the phone and by an app update; Sit Still re-arms them on the next boot, but if a rule missed its window on the day of an update, that is why.

I can’t tell which apps I picked

iPhone. Tap Expand under the row of icons and it opens into a list with names. It is there wherever apps are chosen — the session whitelist, a blocking rule, and the bedtime lock’s let-through list. Sit Still never learns what you picked: iOS hands it a token it can draw and not read, which is why the icons and the names are drawn by the system rather than by the app.

Android. The picker shows names and icons from the start, because Android has no equivalent of those tokens — it simply lets an app ask what is launchable. That does mean the Android app knows which apps you chose. They are kept in its own storage on your phone, they are compared against nothing but the shield you switched on, and nothing about them is written down anywhere else or sent anywhere. The privacy policy says the same in more detail.

My tasks and Reminders disagree — iPhone

The reminder wins on any conflict. Scope is Sit Still’s own Reminders list plus reminders you deliberately linked; a reminder added to another list is not picked up. Deleting travels both ways — deleting a task deletes its reminder, and vice versa.

Do tasks sync with anything — Android

No. Android has no Reminders app to sync with, so the task list stands on its own inside Sit Still. What it has instead is a morning notification that reads out what is due, scheduled and delivered entirely on the phone. It is off unless you turn it on, in Settings → Notifications.

I left a session by accident and lost the time

Leaving takes a deliberate 1.3-second hold precisely so it doesn’t happen by accident, and time left that way is not banked. Finishing early is different: an open-ended session, or a countdown paused past the first minute, banks what you did sit through.

Can I change the pomodoro’s 25 / 5 / 15?

Yes. Settings → Timer → Pomodoro sets the focus block, the short break and the long break a minute at a time — up to two hours for a focus block and an hour for a break — and a reset row appears underneath as soon as you are off the classic numbers. Hold the minus or plus rather than tapping it and the number runs.

A longer break every fourth block is fixed, because that is the shape of the method rather than a setting inside it. New lengths apply to the next session, not the one already on the clock, so a block you are sitting through keeps the numbers it began with. The explanation behind Know more about Pomodoro on the Focus tab always reads out your own three, and says so when they are not the classic ones.

Can I start a session without opening the app?

iPhone. “Start a session in Sit Still”, and the same for ending, pausing and resuming. All four are in the Shortcuts app too, so a session can be one step of an automation — starting when a Focus turns on, for instance. The other direction is not possible: iOS will not let an app switch a Focus on or quiet another app’s notifications, and there is no way around that.

Android. The same four — start, pause, resume and end — sit on the app icon itself: touch and hold Sit Still on the home screen and they are in the menu that opens. There is no Assistant command for them today.

Where are the widgets?

Widgets come with Premium; without it the tile is there but locked. Settings → Widgets → How to add them has instructions with pictures on either phone.

iPhone. Touch and hold the Home Screen, tap the +, and search for Sit Still. There is one for the room you are sitting in with your recent days under it, one for the collection that turns over an hour at a time, and three shapes for your streak on the Lock Screen — the small ones also appear in StandBy while the phone charges on its side.

Android. Touch and hold an empty part of the home screen, tap Widgets, and find Sit Still. There are three: the room you are sitting in with your recent days under it, the collection that turns over an hour at a time, and your streak. Android has no Lock Screen or StandBy equivalent, so those are the three.

How do I earn whiskers, and what are they for?

Two per minute of a completed session, ten more for a session of half an hour or over, and never more than 250 in a day. They buy the two seasonal rooms of the month and, with Premium, things for the cat to wear. Nothing is random — everything is bought outright at a price you can see — and money never buys whiskers.

What does Premium include?

A free account has two rooms and a cat, the countdown and open-ended timers, the focus shield during a session, tasks and the record. Premium opens the rest of the permanent rooms and every cat, and adds the pomodoro, the bedtime lock, focus rules and the widgets. It is monthly or yearly, and Settings → Premium carries both Restore purchases and Manage subscription — which opens the App Store on iPhone and Google Play on Android, since that is where the subscription lives. Seasonal rooms are bought with whiskers either way.

A seasonal room disappeared

Seasonal rooms rotate at the end of the month. One bought with whiskers stays yours for good; the ones you didn’t buy are replaced by the next two, and a room that has left cannot be bought afterwards.

The monthly rooms are the wrong ones for the date

The app checks the date against the network rather than your phone’s clock, so a phone set to the wrong day no longer opens a drop early or hides one that is running. On iPhone that check goes to Apple; on Android it goes to the same address Android itself uses to check whether it has a connection. It needs the network once; a device that has been offline since its clock was changed can be out of step until it next has a connection, and corrects itself when it does. The drop itself still turns over at midnight where you are, so a room arrives in Auckland before it arrives in Los Angeles.

Saving a room as wallpaper does nothing

Save room as wallpaper sits on the Rooms tab, beside the picture it saves.

iPhone. It needs permission to add to Photos, which the phone asks for once. If that was declined, turn it back on in Settings → Privacy & Security → Photos. The app can add images and cannot read your library.

Android. No permission is involved — the picture is written through Android’s own media store, which needs none to add one. It lands in Pictures, so look for it in your gallery there. The app writes the image and reads nothing back.

How do I delete my data?

Delete the app. Everything Sit Still knows lives on the device and goes with it — there is no account and no copy anywhere else.
